Project Information

ArtWorth AI is an intelligent valuation system designed specifically for sculptural artifacts. Unlike conventional price prediction engines, ArtWorth AI integrates multiple dimensions of an artwork, including aesthetic features, historical significance, material quality, and artist reputation. The project’s central aim is to provide a data-driven, transparent, and consistent valuation model that empowers collectors, curators, auction houses, and even casual art investors to understand the relative worth of sculptures. By treating prices as relative indices, our model focuses on ranking and categorization instead of attempting to provide exact monetary currency values, thereby avoiding the pitfalls of inconsistent datasets and undocumented currency units.

Problem Statement

The art market is notorious for its lack of transparency. Prices are influenced not only by intrinsic qualities of the artwork but also by speculative demand, inconsistent documentation, and regional biases. Sculptural artifacts, in particular, suffer from additional challenges: varying material costs, differences in preservation, and the subjective weight of cultural symbolism. Traditional valuation methods rely heavily on expert opinions, which, while valuable, are often subjective, inconsistent, and inaccessible to emerging collectors.

This project addresses these pain points by introducing a machine learning framework that standardizes valuation across multiple features. It bridges the gap between human expertise and data-driven analysis, offering a relative valuation model that categorizes works into Low, Medium, or High tiers of market worth. This democratizes access to art valuation, making it accessible beyond elite circles.

Dataset Used

The dataset powering ArtWorth AI was sourced from curated online repositories and enhanced manually to include aesthetic descriptors, provenance, and artist-level attributes. The dataset covers:

  • Artist reputation scores normalized between 0–1
  • Physical dimensions (height, width, weight)
  • Artwork creation year and derived artwork age
  • Sculpture type and material details
  • Previous auction history and price benchmarks
  • Aesthetic descriptors capturing visual style
  • Provenance data including ownership and exhibition history

Although absolute currency information is missing, our approach treats all numeric values as relative indicators, allowing the model to focus on proportional differences across works. This ensures the predictions remain meaningful even in the absence of standardized currencies.

Machine Learning Models

Two machine learning regressors were evaluated: Random Forest Regressor and Gradient Boosting Regressor. Both were integrated within a preprocessing pipeline that handled categorical encoding, missing value imputation, and feature scaling.

Random Forest provided robust baseline performance due to its ability to capture non-linear interactions and feature importance. Gradient Boosting, on the other hand, offered finer control and often outperformed in terms of R² score on the validation split. GridSearchCV was employed to fine-tune hyperparameters for both models. Ultimately, the best-performing pipeline was saved as the deployable model.

System Workflow

  • Step 1: User enters sculpture attributes (dimensions, material, artist details) into the web form.
  • Step 2: Data is validated, processed, and transformed into numerical and categorical features.
  • Step 3: The trained ML model predicts a relative index value.
  • Step 4: Index value is mapped into a Low/Medium/High valuation tier.
  • Step 5: Confidence scores are generated using ensemble dispersion or perturbation testing.
  • Step 6: Results are displayed with tier explanations, influencing factors, and practical tips.

Explainability & Visualizations

To ensure trust and usability, ArtWorth AI provides confidence estimates alongside each prediction. For ensemble models like Random Forest, standard deviation across trees is translated into a confidence score. For other models, perturbation-based stability testing is applied.

In addition, visualization modules (accessible through the Visualize tab) provide insights into:

  • Accuracy vs. loss trends during training
  • Model confidence across predictions
  • Comparisons between predicted and actual indices
These visualizations help stakeholders understand not only the prediction itself but also the reliability of the prediction.

Real-World Applications

ArtWorth AI has significant applications across multiple sectors:

  • Auction Houses: Pre-screening sculptures and providing estimated valuation tiers.
  • Museums & Galleries: Understanding relative importance of artifacts in exhibitions.
  • Private Collectors: Making informed purchasing or selling decisions with confidence scores.
  • Academic Research: Providing data-driven insight into valuation patterns across cultures.
  • Insurance Companies: Assigning relative risk/coverage policies based on artwork tiering.

By bridging art expertise with machine learning, the project contributes to market transparency, collector empowerment, and preservation of cultural heritage.
