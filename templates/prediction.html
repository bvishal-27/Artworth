Artwork Valuation — Input Form

Fill the form below with artwork details. The system will return a Relative Value Index (abstract score), a tier (Low / Medium / High) and a confidence score.

Notes:
  • All numeric inputs are treated as relative values (units assumed consistent across the dataset).
  • Price fields are not required for prediction by users — they are used internally during training.

Feature-by-Feature Guide

Understand what each field means and why it matters for valuation.

Artist Name

Categorical identifier. Some artists consistently command higher valuations. Encoded as frequency/target-based signals.

Artist Reputation

Normalized score (0–1). Higher reputation usually boosts artwork value significantly.

Sculpture Type

Style or category (e.g., Classical, Modern). Certain types historically achieve higher auction values.

Material

Key intrinsic feature. Bronze and Marble are premium materials, while others may be grouped into "Other".

Dimensions (Height/Width)

Larger sculptures generally fetch higher values. Units not defined, but treated as consistent relative values.

Weight

Relative indicator of density and material cost. Heavy + expensive material may boost valuation.

Creation Year

Year artwork was made. Often linked to artistic movements or historical significance.

Artwork Age

Older works may be more valuable, though the relationship is non-linear (too recent or too old varies).

Previous Auction Price

Strongest predictor of future price. Enter 0 if unknown.

Artist Alive

If the artist is deceased, scarcity often increases value. Encoded as binary feature.

Aesthetic Descriptor

Subjective labels (Elegant, Intricate, etc.). These stylistic tags correlate with valuation trends.

Provenance

Origin/history of the artwork. Museum provenance or prestigious auction houses greatly increase value.
